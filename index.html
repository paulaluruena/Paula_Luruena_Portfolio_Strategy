Profile

A little about how I got here. The full story is on its own page.

Psychology is how I understand people. Serving them well is why I do this work.

For as long as I can remember, I have wanted to know what happens underneath behavior: why we react before we reflect, how we form opinions, what makes us change our minds. Psychology gave that curiosity a method, and a turning point as a teenager gave it a purpose.

I was raised in Madrid, continued my secondary education in England, and completed it in the United States before coming to Tulane. Moving between cultures taught me that behavior only makes sense in context, which is exactly how I approach organizations.

Strategy consulting is where my threads meet: research rigor from a developmental lab, hands-on behavior change, early exposure to how companies gather feedback from their people, and a growing fluency in AI.

Interests

Psychology is the center of everything I do. These are the fields where I want to explore behavior; the first is my main focus.

The psychology of how organizations work

Every operating model is, in the end, a set of human behaviors.

I want to help organizations understand the people inside them: why talent stays, grows, or leaves, how roles and structures shape performance, and what makes a transformation take hold rather than stall. This is where industrial-organizational psychology meets functional strategy.

How psychology connects to this

Questions I’d explore

  • What makes employees adopt a new operating model instead of working around it?
  • Which people signals predict whether an HR modernization will succeed?

The psychology of adoption

Technology only transforms a function when people trust it.

I want to explore how teams adopt AI tools, including agentic AI: what earns trust, what triggers resistance, and how work can be redesigned so people and technology each do what they do best.

Questions I’d explore

  • What makes employees trust an AI tool enough to change how they work?
  • How should roles evolve when routine tasks are automated?

The psychology of choice
